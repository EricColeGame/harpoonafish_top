Your dive in Harpoon a Fish rarely ends because you ran out of harpoons — it ends because your bag hit its limit or your weight kept you pinned in the shallows. This Harpoon a Fish diving guide fixes both problems in the order the game actually cares about: weight first, gear second, bag space third. ## Why Weight, Not Harpoon Power, Controls Your Depth

Harpoon a Fish is a shallow-to-abyss spearfishing sim on Roblox, developed by the group FirstTry and live since late August 2026. You spawn on a small island with a shop, a crate bench, an index board, and a dining table. Everything else is water, and the water is sorted into layers.

The depth gauge runs from 0 to 1,800 m in 300 m bands. The catch that surprises new players: the gauge is gated by **weight**, not by which harpoon you own. The game keeps telling you to eat and sink, and it means it literally. Every fish you swallow adds weight, and weight is what lets you drop into the next band. Buying a better harpoon while sitting at a low weight just means you miss more shots in water you can already reach.

| Depth band | What gates it | What to do before you dive it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–300 m | Tutorial completion | Finish the tutorial dive, then buy the Mukbang table |
| 300–600 m | Early body weight | Eat every common catch instead of selling it |
| 600–900 m | Weight plus a second harpoon | Farm Wood and Stone, open crates for harpoon drops |
| 900–1,200 m | Sustained eating | Bank food, not just cash, before each run |
| 1,200–1,500 m | Bait coverage | Craft bait aimed at species still missing from your index |
| 1,500–1,800 m | A full kit | Three active harpoon slots and an upgraded bag |

The practical takeaway: treat the Mukbang table as your depth upgrade, and treat harpoons as accuracy upgrades. Players who invert that order spend hours stuck around 500 m wondering why the gauge will not budge.

## Gear That Actually Changes a Dive

Four systems sit between you and the abyss, and only two of them are worth early money. Community reports consistently put bag space and harpoon slots above bait, because bait changes *what* bites while bag space decides *how long* you get to keep biting.

| System | Where it comes from | Ceiling | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Harpoons | Tutorial harpoon, then crate drops | Up to three active equip slots | High, once weight is moving |
| Bag space | Starts as a small counter like 1/5 | Ends the dive the moment it fills | Highest |
| Bait | Crafted at the beach bench | One recipe per target species | Medium |
| Crates | Wood and Stone, opened with keys | Harpoons, resources, supplies | Medium |
| Aquarium | One pet slot at the start | Evolved pets paying cash every second | Long-term |

Any solid Harpoon a Fish diving guide will tell you the same thing about bag space: it is a timer, not a stat. A 1/5 counter means five catches per dive, full stop. That makes every shot a spending decision, which is why the next two sections matter more than raw firepower.

Bait deserves one note. The beach bench recipes list the fish each bait still needs, so bait is really an index-completion tool. If your bag is small, do not craft bait for a species you can already catch — you are spending a slot on a fish you have already logged.

## The Dive Loop, Step by Step

The loop is short, but the order of operations decides whether a run pays. Here is the sequence that works, based on the game's own tutorial flow plus player experience from the first few months of release.

| Step | Action | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Check the catch card before you fire | Rarity and weight tell you if the fish is worth a bag slot |
| 2 | Aim and fire | A miss costs time, and time underwater is limited by bag space anyway |
| 3 | Reel using the tutorial timing | Reel timing is the one skill the tutorial teaches before money is at stake |
| 4 | Watch the line, not just the fish | A hooked fish is not a caught fish — another diver can take it |
| 5 | Eat at the Mukbang table | Weight is the only reliable route to deeper bands |
| 6 | Log records at the index board | One world record per species, and it persists |
| 7 | Leave whenever you want | Pets, cash, and index all persist between sessions |

Step 4 is where most new players lose money. The steal mechanic lets any diver harpoon a fish that is already on your line, and whoever lands it keeps the trophy. There is no partial credit and no compensation. If you see another diver drifting toward your reel, either finish the catch fast or accept that you are now racing them.

That single rule is the most important line in this Harpoon a Fish diving guide for anyone who plays on a busy server: never assume a fish is yours until the catch card pops.

## Catch Cards, Rarity, and Bag Space Math

The index tracks 1,800+ species, and every one of them has a catch card showing rarity and weight, plus a record for the heaviest specimen caught. That card is the only information you need to make a keep-or-eat decision.

| Catch card signal | Keep it? | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Species not yet in your index | Yes | Index entries are permanent and never lost |
| Heaviest of that species so far | Yes | Records are tracked per species |
| High rarity, low weight | Usually yes | Rarity drives value more than size at low weight |
| Duplicate common at small size | Eat it | Frees the slot and adds weight for the next band |
| A fish another diver hooked | Steal it if you can land it | Whoever lands it keeps it, no exceptions |

The math is simple once you accept it. With five slots, keeping three duplicates of a common fish costs you 60% of your dive. Eating those duplicates converts a wasted run into a weight gain, and weight is what opens the next 300 m band. The best players in Harpoon a Fish treat their bag as a ledger, not a trophy case.

## Between Dives: Keys, Crates, and Aquarium Income

Downtime on the island is not downtime. Three systems keep working while you are not fishing.

**Ruby Keys** are the crate currency, and the game does not sell them. You earn them from reward claims and from live codes — player reports list a code that pays out 3 Ruby Keys, though codes rotate, so verify any code in-game before assuming it still works. Keys open crates built from Wood and Stone at the bench, and crates are where extra harpoon slots come from.

**The aquarium** starts with a single pet slot and scales into a passive income stream. Evolved pets pay cash every second, including while you are offline, though offline earnings run about 20% slower than online ones. For a player who cannot sit in a server all day, that is the difference between a hobby and a steady drip of cash.

**Badges** — 12 of them at the time of writing — act as a rough completion checklist. If you are unsure what to chase next, the badge list is a better roadmap than the shop.

| Offline activity | What it produces | Rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aquarium pets | Cash per second | ~20% slower than online |
| Index progress | Nothing — index only moves while diving | N/A |
| Weight | Nothing — weight is gained by eating | N/A |
| Crates | Nothing until you open them | N/A |

## Five Mistakes That Keep Divers Shallow

Most stalled progress in Harpoon a Fish traces back to the same handful of habits. Fixing them costs nothing but attention.

| Mistake | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Diving on an empty stomach | Eat before every run — weight is the depth key |
| Firing at every shadow | Read the catch card first, then commit |
| Filling the bag with commons | Eat duplicates instead of hauling them home |
| Ignoring the crate bench | Wood and Stone turn into keys, keys turn into harpoons |
| Fighting a rival for a hooked fish you cannot win | Reel immediately or cut your losses and move on |

One more habit worth building: end dives on purpose. Because nothing is lost when you leave, there is no penalty for surfacing early with four good fish instead of five mediocre ones. A short, high-value run beats a long, low-value one every time — and this Harpoon a Fish diving guide would be incomplete without saying so plainly.

## FAQ

**How deep can you actually dive in Harpoon a Fish?**
The gauge tops out at 1,800 m, split into 300 m bands. Reaching the bottom requires sustained eating for weight plus a full kit of three harpoon slots and an expanded bag. There is no shortcut around the weight gate.

**Is there a beginner-friendly Harpoon a Fish diving guide for contested catches?**
Yes — the short version is that any diver can harpoon a fish already on your line, and whoever lands it keeps it. Fire, reel fast, and never assume a hooked fish is a caught one. If a rival closes in, finish the catch or move to a quieter spot.

**Do you lose anything when you leave the server?**
No. Pets, cash, and your fish index all persist between sessions, and aquarium pets keep earning while you are offline at roughly 80% of the online rate. Your weight and depth progress are tied to your character, so there is nothing to protect by staying.

**How do you get Ruby Keys without spending Robux?**
Keys come from reward claims and from live codes rather than from the shop. Community reports mention a code paying 3 Ruby Keys, but codes expire, so test any code in-game rather than trusting a list.
