## The Two-Phase Mechanic Behind Harpoon a Fish Catching Fish

On PC, the opening tutorial drops an arrow over one specific fish. You move the cursor onto that target, click to fire, and the game immediately switches modes: a reel prompt appears, and the outcome is decided by how you handle that prompt rather than by the shot itself. A clean hit feels like success, but it only buys you the attempt.

That distinction is the entire skill floor. Community reports describe the reel as a timed input — you are reading the prompt, not the fish. Beginners who keep adjusting their aim during the reel phase tend to lose catches they had already earned. The moment the harpoon fires, your job changes completely.

| Phase | PC input | What it decides | What failure looks like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aim | Move the cursor onto the marked fish | Whether an attempt can start | The shot travels past the target |
| Fire | Click | Whether the reel prompt opens | No prompt appears, no catch |
| Reel | Timed pull on the prompt | Whether the fish actually lands | The fish escapes after a clean hit |

Small catches are the right classroom. A shrimp teaches you aiming, impact feedback, and the shape of a successful catch without layering on a collection goal. Chasing the biggest silhouette on screen during your second minute stacks a hard target on top of an unfamiliar input sequence — a reliable way to end a session with zero fish in the bag.

## The Tutorial Catch, Step by Step

The sequence is short, but each step feeds the next one. Player experience suggests running it exactly as presented the first time, then experimenting once a fish is actually on the line.

| Step | What you do | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Follow the tutorial arrow into the water | The game marks a valid, easy target for you |
| 2 | Aim at the highlighted fish | Confirms you are tracking the right object |
| 3 | Click to fire the harpoon | Starts the attempt and opens the reel prompt |
| 4 | Watch the reel prompt and time the pull | This is where the catch is genuinely won |
| 5 | Press Space to return to the island (PC) | Brings the catch back into the progression loop |
| 6 | Follow the next island arrow task | Keeps the tutorial chain intact instead of stalling |

Step five is the one players skip most often. A landed fish sitting in the water does nothing for you; the value only appears once it is back on land and pushed into the aquarium or a quest slot. Treat the return trip as part of the catch, not as downtime.

## The Island Loop: Turning Catches Into Pets and Depth

Back on the island, the tutorial keeps handing you arrow tasks — and those tasks are the actual progression system. Caught fish can become aquarium pets, and the developer has tied the aquarium directly to growing your new catch. That gives every fish a second purpose beyond a single successful reel.

The other half of the loop is the Mukbang table. When the task appears, buy the table, sit, and complete the eating interaction. Eating raises your character's weight, and weight is what lets you sink deeper. This is a genuinely separate axis of progress: a better shot solves accuracy, while a heavier character solves reach. You need both, and they improve through different actions.

| Task | Action | Useful result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First catch | Aim, fire, and time the reel | A landed fish rather than a hit |
| First pet | Return to the island and place the catch in the aquarium | A pet you can grow |
| Deeper reach | Buy the Mukbang table and complete the eating step | Character-weight progression |
| Wider loadout | Unlock and use equipment slots | More of your owned harpoons fielded at once |

Equipment slots matter more than they look. Community reports note that slots let you bring multiple harpoons you already own into the water, which quietly changes how many attempts a single dive can absorb. If you keep running dry on shots before you run out of fish, that is a slot problem, not an aim problem.

## How Depth Reshapes Harpoon a Fish Catching Fish

Depth is the mechanic that makes Harpoon a Fish catching fish feel different at hour five than it did at minute five. Every fish you can reach is gated by two things: how far down your character can sink, and what your harpoon setup can handle at that range.

| Depth stage | What gates it | What changes | Practical tip |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shallow tutorial water | Nothing — it is scripted | Slow, forgiving targets | Learn the reel rhythm here |
| Near-shore water | Basic gear | More species, similar timing | Practice aim before firing |
| Mid depth | Character weight from eating | Longer dives, less room for error | Bank weight before pushing lower |
| Deep water | Weight plus a stronger loadout | High-value targets, tighter reels | Bring spare harpoons via equipment slots |

The pattern to internalize: weight is a currency you spend on access, not a stat you max for its own sake. If a new depth keeps ending in empty dives, check whether you are actually heavy enough to stay there long enough to line up a shot.

## Spending Early Money, Rewards, and Codes

New players tend to spread their first earnings across whatever button looks most exciting, which usually leaves a tutorial task unfinished. A tighter approach: fund the tutorial first, then optional purchases.

- **Money** — route it into the table and any task the arrow is currently pointing at. The table introduces eating, and eating unlocks depth.
- **Playtime Rewards** — the demonstrated rewards include XP and potions. Claim them while you are on the island rather than mid-dive.
- **Code rewards** — the creator's code reward includes Ruby Keys, which is a different resource from money and from XP.
- **Bait recipes and index entries** — these are mid-term goals, not opening purchases. They become useful once you can reliably land a fish.

Keep those resource names separate in your head. A reward is only useful when you know which menu consumes it — knowing that you received Ruby Keys is not the same as knowing where they are spent, and it is definitely not a reason to assume every crate on the map is now openable.

## Setting One Clear Goal for Your Next Dive

Once you have landed a catch and placed a pet, the open world gets a lot less helpful — nobody tells you what to chase next. Give each dive exactly one purpose:

- A specific fish requested by a bait recipe you are working toward.
- A missing index entry you have already seen but not landed.
- A catch from a depth you just unlocked through weight gain.
- A test run of a new harpoon after opening an equipment slot.

A single stated target turns an ordinary dive into measurable progress. Rotating between every visible objective is what makes sessions feel busy but unproductive.

## FAQ

**Does a perfect shot guarantee the catch in Harpoon a Fish catching fish?**
No. The shot only opens the attempt. Landing the fish depends on the timed reel prompt, so a slightly off-center hit that you reel cleanly beats a perfect shot you fumble.

**How do I unlock deeper water?**
Through character weight, which comes from the eating interaction at the Mukbang table. Depth access and harpoon accuracy are separate progression tracks, so improving one will not fix the other.

**What should I do with my very first fish?**
Bring it back to the island with Space and place it in the aquarium so it becomes a pet. A fish left unreturned does nothing for your progression.

**Do codes and Playtime Rewards matter early on?**
They help, but they are not a shortcut past the basics. Claim them on the island, note whether you received XP, potions, or Ruby Keys, and keep working through the arrow tasks — the tutorial chain is still the fastest route to a working routine.

Get the two-phase rhythm down, return every catch to the island, and let weight open the map for you. That is the whole loop — and once it clicks, Harpoon a Fish catching fish stops feeling like a dice roll and starts feeling like a skill you can practice.
