### Reading the Water Before You Fire

Before you fire a single harpoon, spend a few seconds watching how a creature moves. Some swim in straight lines, some drift lazily near the surface, and some dart unpredictably. That pattern is your real difficulty rating — a slow, predictable creature in a deep zone is often an easier catch than a fast one near the surface. Community reports consistently suggest that patience at this stage pays off more than gear does.

## Aim, Fire, and Reel: The Harpoon Fundamentals

This is the section that decides whether you enjoy the game. Everything else — codes, evolution, aquarium management — is support for the moment your harpoon connects.

| Mechanic | What Good Looks Like | Common Mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Aiming | Lead the target slightly ahead of its movement | Firing directly at where the fish *is* |
| Firing | Short, confident shots at close-to-mid range | Long desperation shots at distant targets |
| Reeling | Steady input with small corrections | Panic-tapping and losing tension |
| Positioning | Stay above or beside the target, not below it | Chasing from underneath and losing sight |
| Recovery | Reset after a miss instead of spamming | Wasting the next three shots in frustration |

### The Three-Second Rule

Give yourself three seconds before every shot. One second to watch the creature's pattern, one second to line up the lead, and one second to commit. It sounds slow, but it cuts your miss rate dramatically, and fewer misses means more catches per dive. Experienced players do this automatically; beginners have to practice it deliberately.

### Reeling Is a Skill, Not a Formality

Landing the harpoon is only half the catch. Reeling is where the creature can still escape, and it is where most new players lose their best finds. The trick is to treat the reel like a conversation — apply pressure, ease off when the line strains, then apply pressure again. Constant maximum tension snaps or loses catches; zero tension lets them swim free.

If you are struggling with **Harpoon a Fish how to play** at the reeling stage, drop back to shallow water and practice on common fish until the rhythm feels natural. There is no penalty for practicing, and the muscle memory transfers directly to rarer catches later.

Keep one display copy if you are sentimental, then feed the rest into evolution.
