That means "getting better fish" is really two jobs: descend into richer pools, then build enough power to survive and land what lives there.

## Reel Timing and Aim: The Skills Nobody Explains

Gear gets you into the pool; execution decides what leaves it. Two players with identical harpoons can have wildly different results purely based on how they aim and reel.

### Aim at silhouettes, not at the school

Bigger creatures read as larger, slower-moving silhouettes against the background. Firing into a dense school of small fish wastes a shot and often triggers a common catch. Track the outlier — the shape that doesn't match the swarm — and lead it slightly if it's moving laterally.

### Reel in a rhythm, not in a panic

- Keep the tension bar inside the safe band instead of tapping it to the edge.
- Ease off the moment the bar turns red, then resume — the catch stays hooked.
- Don't spam the fire button; one clean shot on the right target beats three rushed ones.
- Watch the creature's movement pattern before firing, not after.

| Reel mistake | Symptom | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Holding reel constantly | Tension snaps, fish escapes | Pulse the reel in short bursts |
| Firing at the closest fish | Endless common catches | Target the largest silhouette |
| Chasing a rare spawn you can't damage | Repeated escapes, wasted time | Upgrade harpoon, then return |
| Ignoring the tension color change | Losses at 90% completion | React to the bar, not the fish |

## Evolution and Aquarium Management: Turning Good Fish Into Great Ones

A rare catch is only half the job. The other half is what you do with it afterward — and this is where a lot of players quietly lose progress in **Harpoon a Fish how to get better fish** terms without realizing it.

| Catch situation | Best action | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First time catching a species | Keep it in the aquarium | Collection progress and evolution chains |
| Duplicate of a common species | Feed into evolution | Frees slots and pushes tiers up |
| Duplicate of a rare species | Evolve, but keep one | Rarity is hard to replace later |
| Fish that sits in a chain you're pushing | Prioritize it | Chains compound into bigger monsters |
| Low-tier filler fish | Clear out first | Wasted aquarium space |

The evolution system is the reason duplicates matter. Instead of treating a second copy of a common fish as junk, treat it as fuel: it moves something else up a tier, and higher tiers are what actually count as "better fish" in your collection. Community reports suggest keeping a clean one-of-everything shelf while funneling every spare duplicate into evolution — that keeps collection progress and power growth moving in parallel.

## FAQ

**Is Harpoon a Fish how to get better fish mostly about luck?**
No. The creature pool is tied to your depth, and your harpoon damage decides whether deep-zone creatures can be landed. Better gear plus deeper water produces better catches far more reliably than repeating shallow runs.

**How often should I move to a deeper zone?**
Whenever catches in your current zone land in one shot with no tension, or when your aquarium is filling with duplicates you've already evolved. Both signals mean you've out-leveled the pool.

**Do codes actually help me catch better fish?**
They help indirectly. EXP levels you faster so stronger harpoons unlock sooner, and Ruby Keys unlock rewards that stock your aquarium with evolution material. The catching itself still comes down to depth and gear.

**Should I keep or evolve my rare duplicates?**
Keep one of each rare species for collection and evolution chains, then evolve the rest. Commons, on the other hand, are almost always better used as evolution fuel than stored.
